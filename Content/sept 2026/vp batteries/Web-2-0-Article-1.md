## Causes of Premature Battery Deterioration in Classic and Weekend Cruisers

Many American cars in New Zealand are kept as weekend cruisers or enthusiast vehicles rather than daily drivers. Extended storage periods are among the primary causes of premature battery degradation. Over time, parasitic electrical drains from clocks, alarm systems, and onboard computers slowly discharge the battery.

When a lead-acid battery remains in a discharged state, lead sulphate crystals gradually harden on the internal plates. This process, known as sulfation, diminishes the active surface area and permanently reduces cold cranking capacity. Installing a smart maintenance charger during storage helps counteract parasitic discharge and preserves battery life.

Physical vibration is another major factor in performance degradation. Stiff performance suspensions and uneven road surfaces transfer shock loads into the battery casing. Heavy-duty internal plate anchoring prevents internal short circuits caused by mechanical vibration.

## Best Practices for Long-Term Battery Reliability

Proactive maintenance safeguards vehicle reliability and extends service life. Vehicle owners should routinely inspect battery terminals for signs of corrosion or loose connections. White or bluish powder around the terminals indicates acid vapour venting, which can be neutralised with warm water and a wire brush.

Applying protective terminal grease after cleaning prevents future oxidation and maintains optimal conductivity. Periodically testing resting voltage with a multimeter provides an early indication of battery degradation before complete failure occurs. A fully charged battery should display approximately 12.6 volts when the engine is off.
