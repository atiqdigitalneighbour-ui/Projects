## The Distinct Characteristics of Canterbury Concrete Substrates

Concrete poured throughout Christchurch and surrounding districts features a unique blend of local alluvial river gravels, greywacke stones, and quartz sands. These river stones provide exceptional compressive strength and distinctive visual appeal when exposed through diamond grinding. However, their density and varied hardness also demand precise mechanical tooling and expert calibration.

When grinding Canterbury slabs, technicians must select appropriate diamond bond matrices to cleanly cut through hard river stones without dislodging them from the cement paste. Uneven grinding or improper tooling can cause aggregate plucking, leaving unsightly surface voids that compromise the final aesthetic.

## Mechanical Polishing vs Topical Floor Coatings

A critical distinction in concrete surface finishing exists between true Mechanically Polished Concrete (MPC) and sacrificial topical coatings, such as grind-and-seal systems. Topical sealers rely on a thin layer of clear acrylic or polyurethane laid over the surface. While initial costs may appear lower, topical films inevitably scratch, scuff, and wear away under foot traffic, requiring frequent stripping and reapplication.

In contrast, Mechanically Polished Concrete does not rely on a topical plastic film to generate shine or provide protection. Technicians refine the slab through 15 to 20 successive passes with industrial planetary grinding machinery, progressing from coarse metal-bond diamonds to ultra-fine resin pads. During this sequence, liquid silicate densifiers are applied to penetrate the concrete pores and trigger a chemical crystallization reaction.

## Hypoallergenic Benefits and Indoor Air Quality

Maintaining healthy indoor air quality represents a primary concern for modern families, especially during damp winter months. Traditional carpets trap dust mites, pet dander, pollen, and airborne moisture, creating persistent challenges for individuals suffering from asthma or respiratory allergies.

Polished concrete provides an entirely non-porous, monolithic surface that cannot harbour biological contaminants. The mechanical densification process seals microscopic pores, preventing mould and mildew spores from taking root within the slab. Dust particles remain on the surface, where they can be effortlessly collected with a dry microfibre mop.

Furthermore, mechanical concrete polishing produces zero volatile organic compound (VOC) emissions during and after installation. The densifiers and penetrating protectors used are water-based and odourless, ensuring that newly polished homes remain safe and comfortable for immediate occupancy.
