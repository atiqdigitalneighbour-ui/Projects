# Architectural Finishes in Concrete Surface Design: Specifying Sheen Levels and Aggregate Exposure for High-End Commercial and Residential Builds

The evolution of modern architectural surface design has established exposed concrete as one of the most expressive, durable, and versatile building materials available. Once relegated to utilitarian subfloors and industrial warehouses, concrete is now celebrated in premier commercial developments, luxury residential residences, and public art galleries. Its organic texture, structural permanence, and understated sophistication make it an ideal canvas for contemporary interior concepts.

However, specifying a finished concrete floor is far more nuanced than simply selecting a tile colour or timber stain. Concrete is a natural, dynamic composite material, and no two slabs are entirely identical. Achieving a bespoke aesthetic requires architects, interior designers, and property owners to understand the technical interplay between aggregate exposure depth, chemical densification, and surface sheen levels.

By learning how different mechanical grinding regimes dictate final appearance and surface performance, specifiers can craft tailored flooring environments that align perfectly with their architectural vision.

---

## The Spectrum of Aggregate Exposure: From Cream Polish to Full River Stone

The depth to which a concrete slab is mechanically ground determines the volume and character of the aggregate revealed. When fresh concrete is placed and screeded, heavier stone aggregates naturally settle toward the lower third of the slab, while a fine paste of cement, water, and sand rises to the top, forming the surface laitance.

Specifiers can select from several standardised aggregate exposure levels to achieve their desired design aesthetic:

### 1. Natural Cream Finish (Zero Aggregate Exposure)
The natural cream finish represents the most subtle concrete aesthetic. Technicians avoid aggressive metal-bond grinding, instead utilising fine ceramic and resin pads to lightly polish the uppermost cement paste without cutting into the underlying stone. This results in a smooth, cloud-like surface with gentle tonal variations, characteristic mottling, and trowel swirl marks. It creates an understated, industrial-chic appearance ideal for modern minimalist interiors, though it requires an impeccably poured, flat slab free from surface rain damage.

### 2. Salt-and-Pepper Finish (Fine Aggregate Exposure)
Achieved by grinding roughly one millimetre into the surface paste, a salt-and-pepper finish exposes fine sand particles and tiny aggregate specks. This exposure level delivers a balanced, textured look that masks minor surface imperfections without revealing large river stones. It represents an exceptionally popular choice for contemporary homes, retail fashion boutiques, and modern office spaces.

### 3. Medium to Full Aggregate Exposure
To expose large, dense river stones and basalt gravels, technicians grind three to five millimetres into the cured slab using coarse metal-bond diamond tooling. In the Canterbury region, this reveals a stunning mosaic of local greywacke stones, smooth quartz pebbles, and dark volcanic aggregates. A full aggregate grind delivers dramatic visual depth, reminiscent of classic Italian terrazzo, while providing maximum surface abrasion resistance for high-traffic environments.

## Calibrating Surface Sheen: Matte, Satin, and High-Gloss Optics

Following aggregate exposure, the next major design decision involves specifying the mechanical gloss level. In true mechanically polished concrete, gloss is not produced by rolling on a shiny varnish or polyurethane glaze. Rather, the sheen is achieved through progressive diamond refinement that cuts microscopic facets into the hardened mineral matrix.

Specifiers can choose between distinct sheen profiles based on ambient lighting and functional requirements:

### Matte Finish (100 to 200-Grit Refinement)
A matte polish provides a flat, velvety surface that diffuses light rather than reflecting it. It produces a raw, organic stone feel that works well in rustic architectural designs, outdoor covered loggias, and spaces where high surface glare would be undesirable.

### Satin Sheen (400 to 800-Grit Refinement)
Satin represents the most versatile and widely specified sheen level in residential construction. Refined to 400 or 800 grit, the floor produces a warm, low-sheen glow that gently reflects natural daylight from windows without creating sharp glare. It offers an exceptionally forgiving finish that conceals daily dust, footprints, and pet hair, making it the practical choice for busy family living.

### High-Gloss Polish (1,500 to 3,000-Grit Refinement)
For projects demanding maximum visual drama and light reflectivity, high-gloss mechanical polishing delivers an extraordinary, mirror-like clarity. Refined through ultra-fine 1,500 and 3,000-grit diamond resin pucks, the floor clearly reflects ceiling lights, wall artwork, and interior architecture.

High-gloss polished concrete provides distinct practical benefits in commercial environments, such as retail supermarkets, vehicle showrooms, and corporate lobbies. The luminous floor bounces natural and artificial illumination throughout the interior volume, significantly reducing electrical lighting power consumption and creating an open, expansive atmosphere.

Architectural polished concrete stands as a premier example of sustainable material stewardship. By refining the structural foundation slab that is already present in the building, projects eliminate the embodied energy, transport emissions, and synthetic adhesives associated with supplementary floor coverings.
