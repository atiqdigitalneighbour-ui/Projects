## Working with Regional Alluvial Aggregates and Seismic Dynamics

Concrete poured throughout the Canterbury region is characterised by a dense matrix of hard river gravels, greywacke stones, and volcanic basalts. These alluvial stones possess high compressive strength and display striking natural colour variations when cut and exposed through diamond grinding.

However, the hardness of local river gravels requires specialised machinery and precise tooling selection. Technicians must match the diamond matrix bond to the hardness of Canterbury aggregates. Utilising incorrect tooling bonds can dislodge stones from the cement paste, creating surface gouges known as aggregate plucking.

Furthermore, regional seismic history means many existing concrete foundations exhibit hairline settlement cracks or surface undulations. Professional surface preparation addresses these imperfections through diamond V-routing, low-viscosity structural epoxy injection, and polymer pore grouting.

## Thermal Mass and Energy Conservation in Canterbury Climates

One of the most valuable characteristics of polished concrete in Canterbury is its high thermal mass. Concrete possesses an exceptional ability to absorb, store, and release heat energy.

In winter, properly positioned polished concrete floors absorb passive solar warmth entering through north-facing glazing during daylight hours. As night approaches and exterior temperatures decline, the floor slowly releases its stored thermal energy, moderating indoor temperatures and lowering domestic heating requirements.

Polished concrete also performs with unmatched efficiency when paired with in-slab hydronic heating. Because concrete transfers heat directly into the living volume without the thermal resistance created by carpet underlays or timber boards, heating circuits reach target temperatures faster and consume significantly less energy.
