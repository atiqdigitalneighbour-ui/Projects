## Evaluating Multi-Sector Routing Corridors

Because commercial airlines do not operate direct flights between Australasia and Somalia, passengers must select flight corridors that route through primary intermediate hubs in the Middle East or East Africa before connecting to Aden Adde International Airport in Mogadishu.

The most reliable and well-established routing corridors transit through major Arabian Gulf hubs such as Dubai or Doha, or via key African crossroads such as Addis Ababa or Nairobi. Connecting through Gulf hubs enables travellers to complete the initial ultra-long-haul flight aboard premier full-service carriers, followed by a direct or single-stop regional service into Mogadishu. Alternatively, transiting through African aviation centres such as Addis Ababa provides seamless connectivity via continental network carriers with extensive regional route networks.

However, selecting transit hubs requires careful evaluation of layover durations. Because flights into Mogadishu typically operate on dedicated daylight arrival schedules, timing intermediate connections appropriately is essential. Scheduling layovers of less than three hours introduces severe operational risk, particularly when passengers must transfer between international carriers or change airport terminals. Layovers between three and five hours provide an optimal safety margin, allowing travellers to complete security screenings and navigate gate transfers without haste.
