# What Separates a Quality Townhouse Developer from the Rest in Perth

Choosing a townhouse developer is not the same as choosing a builder for a custom home. When a buyer purchases a townhouse from a developer, they are entrusting the entire design, approval, construction, and delivery process to that organisation. Understanding what distinguishes a quality developer from a substandard one can protect buyers from expensive mistakes and a great deal of unnecessary stress.

This article outlines the markers buyers should look for when assessing townhouse developers in Perth's current market.

---

## Track Record Is the First Filter

A developer's track record is the most reliable indicator of future performance. Marketing materials, renders, and showrooms can be produced by any business regardless of its actual delivery capability. Completed projects cannot be faked.

Buyers should ask for a list of completed developments and, where possible, arrange to view the finished product. Look for consistency across multiple projects, not a single flagship development that may not reflect the standard applied across the portfolio. Ask specifically how many projects have been completed, delivered on time, and sold out.

In Perth's market, where several builders and developers have entered administration in recent years, the ability to point to a sustained track record of solvent completion is a meaningful differentiator. Buyers who skip this check are accepting a risk they may not fully appreciate until it becomes a problem.

---

## Licensing and Regulatory Compliance

In Western Australia, residential construction must be carried out under a valid builder's licence issued by the Building and Energy division of the Department of Mines, Industry Regulation and Safety. Buyers can verify whether a builder or developer holds a current licence through the public licence register.

A developer who is managing a project but subcontracting all construction should still be able to confirm the licence status of the builder carrying out the work. If a developer cannot or will not provide this information, that is a significant warning sign.

---

## Contract Transparency: The Fixed Price Test

Perth's property market has been marked by contract disputes arising from provisional sum arrangements and escalation clauses that were not clearly disclosed to buyers at the time of signing. A quality developer offers a genuinely fixed-price contract with no hidden variations.

Ask specifically:

- Is the price fixed from exchange to settlement?
- Are there any provisional sums in the contract?
- Are there any price escalation or cost variation clauses?
- What happens if material costs increase during construction?

A developer who cannot confirm a fully fixed price in writing should be treated with caution. The financial exposure from open-ended provisional sums and variation clauses can add tens of thousands of dollars to the final cost of a purchase.

## Warranty Standards and Post-Handover Accountability

Western Australian law requires residential builders to provide certain minimum warranties under the Home Building Contracts Act. Buyers should confirm that the developer they are considering meets at minimum the statutory requirements and, ideally, offers warranty coverage that exceeds them.

A structural warranty of six years, aligned with Australian Standards, is a useful benchmark for comparison. The scope of the warranty — what structural components it covers and what is excluded — should be provided in writing before any contract is signed.

Ask how defects are reported and resolved after handover. A developer with a formal post-handover support process is operating at a higher standard than one who considers their obligations complete at settlement. The reality is that minor defects in new construction are common, and how a developer manages them is as indicative of quality as the initial construction itself.

## Communication and Access to Decision-Makers

The quality of communication during a development project significantly affects the buyer experience. Buyers who can reach a person with decision-making authority when they have a question or a concern are far less likely to experience prolonged delays in resolution.

Ask prospective developers to describe their communication model. Will you have a named contact person? Can you reach someone by phone or email who can actually answer your question, rather than logging it and promising a follow-up? Can you speak with a director or senior principal if needed?

Boutique developers operating a smaller portfolio of projects typically offer more direct communication channels than large volume builders. This is not a universal rule, but the structural logic is sound: in a smaller organisation, each project and each buyer carries a higher proportion of the company's overall activity, which creates stronger incentives to manage the relationship well.

---

## Inclusions and What Turnkey Actually Delivers

The inclusions schedule is the practical definition of what a buyer receives at settlement. A developer who uses the term "turnkey" but delivers an unlandscaped property without window treatments, floor coverings, or air conditioning is not offering a genuine turnkey product.

Before signing, request the full inclusions schedule in writing and verify that every livability item is covered. The schedule should specify floor covering type, kitchen appliance brands or equivalents, air conditioning type and capacity, and all external works including landscaping, fencing, driveway, and reticulation.

Comparing inclusions schedules between developers on a line-by-line basis is one of the most effective ways to understand the true delivered value of competing developments.
