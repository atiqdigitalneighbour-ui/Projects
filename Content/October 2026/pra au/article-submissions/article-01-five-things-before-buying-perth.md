# Five Things to Check Before Buying a New Property in Perth

Purchasing a new residential property in Perth is a significant financial commitment. Whether you are a first-home buyer entering the market for the first time or an investor adding to an existing portfolio, the due diligence process matters. New properties offer advantages that established homes cannot — modern energy ratings, manufacturer warranties on fixtures, and full depreciation benefits for investors — but they also require buyers to look carefully at aspects that established home purchases do not typically involve.

Here are five areas every buyer should evaluate before signing a contract on a new Perth property.

---

## 1. Location and Suburb Fundamentals

Location is the only element of a property that cannot be changed after purchase. Before considering the dwelling itself, assess the suburb's fundamentals.

Consider proximity to employment precincts. Perth's inner-east suburbs — Rivervale, Belmont, and Kewdale — sit within five to eight kilometres of the CBD. They are also adjacent to Perth Airport, which is one of the city's largest employment nodes. Suburbs that draw from multiple employment sources tend to sustain rental demand more consistently over time.

Transport connections matter for both liveability and rental appeal. Public transport access, road connectivity, and proximity to the METRONET rail expansion routes all affect the pool of tenants and buyers a property will attract in the future.

Lifestyle amenities — proximity to schools, healthcare, shopping, parks, and recreational facilities — contribute to both rental demand and long-term capital growth. Belmont Forum Shopping Centre, the Swan River foreshore, and the Optus Stadium entertainment precinct all sit within or immediately adjacent to the inner-east corridor.

## 2. Contract Inclusions: What Turnkey Really Means

"Turnkey" is one of the most commonly misused terms in Perth's property market. Its true meaning — a property that is fully finished and requires no further expenditure before it is liveable or tenantable — is not universally applied by developers.

Before signing any contract, request a written inclusions schedule. This document should specify every item the buyer will receive at settlement. A complete turnkey package should include:

- Floor coverings throughout (specify the type and grade)
- Kitchen appliances, fully connected to services
- Reverse-cycle air conditioning, installed and operational
- Window treatments
- Landscaping, including reticulation
- Driveway, external paths, and boundary fencing
- Letterbox and clothesline

Any item missing from the inclusions schedule is the buyer's cost to arrange post-settlement. For investors purchasing from interstate, an incomplete property can mean weeks of delay before a tenant can move in and rental income begins.

## 3. Title Type and Strata Structure in Western Australia

Western Australia has three main property title structures relevant to new townhouse and multi-unit developments. Understanding which applies to a property under consideration affects the ongoing costs of ownership.

**Survey-Strata Title** is the most common structure for boutique townhouse developments in Perth. Each owner holds title to their individual surveyed lot. Common property (usually the shared driveway) is co-owned by all lot holders. Strata levies on Survey-Strata developments are typically low, covering shared insurance and minor common area maintenance.

**Green Title (Freehold)** is the most independent ownership structure. The owner holds complete individual title with no shared land and no strata obligations. Green Title properties carry no levies but require a separate land parcel for each dwelling, which is less common in infill developments.

**Strata Title** is the traditional structure for apartment buildings. Owners hold title to their individual lot within the building and share common property such as lobbies, lifts, and recreational facilities. Strata levies in these developments can be substantial if the shared amenities are extensive.

Buyers should confirm the title type early in the assessment process and obtain written confirmation of the levy structure and what those levies cover before signing.

## 4. Construction Warranty and Defect Liability

New properties in Western Australia are backed by statutory warranties under the Home Building Contracts Act. Buyers should confirm the specific warranty terms that apply to any property they are considering before settlement.

A six-year structural warranty covering the structural components of the building is a useful baseline to apply when comparing developers. This warranty period provides meaningful long-term protection against defects that may not become apparent until some time after handover.

In addition to the structural warranty, ask about the defect liability period, during which the developer or builder is responsible for rectifying non-structural defects identified after handover. Understanding the process for reporting and resolving defects — including who to contact and what the expected response time is — helps buyers manage expectations and plan accordingly.

## 5. Settlement Timelines and Off-the-Plan Risks

For buyers purchasing an off-the-plan property — one that is not yet constructed or is under construction at the time of exchange — the settlement timeline introduces specific risks that established home purchases do not carry.

The most important safeguard is the sunset clause: the date by which the development must be completed and the property settled. If the developer cannot deliver by this date, both parties may have the right to rescind the contract and the buyer's deposit is returned. Assess whether the sunset clause date is realistic given the stage of construction and the developer's stated timeline.

Buyers should also confirm that deposits are held in a statutory trust account under the Real Estate and Business Agents Act, as required under Western Australian law. This structure protects buyer funds from being used by the developer for operational expenses during construction.

For buyers who prefer to avoid the uncertainty of off-the-plan purchases entirely, some developers offer completed or near-complete properties that can be inspected before commitment and settled within standard timeframes. This path removes settlement delay risk while still delivering the benefits of new construction.
