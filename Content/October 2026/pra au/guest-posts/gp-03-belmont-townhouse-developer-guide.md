## The Difference Between a Boutique Townhouse Developer and a Volume Builder

The distinction between boutique developers and volume builders is material and not always obvious from marketing materials.

Volume builders operate across a large geographic spread, producing homes at scale using standardised plans and bulk material procurement. Their business model prioritises throughput, which can mean a buyer's project is one of hundreds being managed simultaneously. Communication often passes through layers of administration before reaching anyone who can make a decision.

Boutique residential developers, by contrast, tend to focus on a smaller number of developments within a defined geographic area. Because each project represents a higher proportion of the company's overall activity, the incentive to deliver quality and resolve issues promptly is structurally stronger. Buyers working with boutique developers often have the ability to communicate directly with directors or senior project managers, which changes the nature of the relationship significantly.

Neither model is universally better for every buyer. However, buyers who value personal accountability, faster responses to queries, and the ability to customise certain aspects of their property during construction often find boutique developers better suited to their needs.

## What to Check in a Townhouse Contract

Property contracts in Western Australia are legally binding documents with significant financial consequences. Buyers who do not read and understand the full contract — ideally with the assistance of an independent solicitor — are accepting risks they may not fully appreciate.

### Fixed Price vs Provisional Sums

A fixed-price contract guarantees that the total price paid at settlement will match the price agreed at exchange, subject to any buyer-initiated variations. A contract with provisional sums, by contrast, includes estimate line items that can increase between contract execution and handover.

Common provisional sum items include floor coverings, window treatments, and site-specific costs such as rock removal. Buyers should request that these items be converted to fixed costs before signing, or at minimum, understand the range of possible overruns each provisional sum might generate.

### Inclusions Schedule

Request a detailed inclusions schedule before proceeding. This document should specify, at a minimum:

- Floor covering type and specifications
- Kitchen appliances (make, model or equivalent standard)
- Air conditioning type and capacity
- Window treatments
- External works (landscaping, driveways, fencing, letterbox, clothesline)
- Lighting and electrical fittings

Any item not listed in the inclusions schedule is the buyer's responsibility post-settlement. For investors purchasing from interstate, unspecified inclusions can translate into significant and unexpected costs to make a property tenantable.

### Sunset Clause Date

All off-the-plan contracts in Western Australia should include a sunset clause: a date by which the development must be completed and the property settled. If the developer fails to meet this date, both parties may have the right to rescind the contract and the deposit is returned.

Buyers should assess whether the sunset clause date is realistic given the project's construction schedule. A sunset clause set too close to the expected completion date leaves no buffer for delays. A sunset clause set excessively far in the future gives the developer more flexibility than may be appropriate.

## Understanding Strata Title Types in Western Australia

Western Australia has three principal title structures for multi-unit residential developments, each with distinct implications for ownership costs and obligations.

### Survey-Strata Title

Survey-Strata is the most common title structure for boutique townhouse developments in Perth. Under this structure, each owner holds individual title to their surveyed lot and any improvements on it. Common property (typically the shared driveway) is owned collectively by the owners as a group. Survey-Strata titles typically carry low administrative levies because the shared infrastructure is minimal.

### Green Title (Freehold)

Green Title represents the most independent form of ownership. The owner holds complete individual title to the land and has no shared common property or strata obligations. In the context of multi-unit developments, Green Title requires a separate land parcel for each dwelling, which typically involves a more complex subdivision process. Green Title properties carry no strata levies.

### Strata Title

Strata Title is the traditional multi-unit structure used predominantly in apartment blocks. Strata owners hold title to their individual lot (the airspace their unit occupies) plus a share of the common property. Strata developments are governed by a strata company and incur regular levies covering building insurance, common area maintenance, and administration.

For buyers assessing townhouse developments in Belmont, confirming the applicable title type before signing is important. Boutique townhouse developments are most commonly Survey-Strata, which typically means lower ongoing costs than a traditional Strata structure.

## What Genuine Turnkey Delivery Looks Like

"Turnkey" is used frequently in property marketing but the definition applied by developers varies considerably.

A genuinely turnkey property requires no further expenditure by the buyer before it is liveable or immediately tenantable. That means:

- All internal finishes are complete, including painting, floor coverings, and window treatments
- The kitchen is fully fitted with appliances and connected to services
- Air conditioning is installed and operational
- The garden and courtyard are landscaped, with reticulation operational where specified
- The driveway, external paths, and boundary fencing are complete
- Utility connections (power, water, NBN) are in place

If a developer describes a property as turnkey but the inclusions schedule excludes several of the above items, the property is not fully turnkey. Buyers should require the developer to define in writing precisely what is and is not included before proceeding.

## A Final Consideration: Post-Handover Support

The relationship with a developer does not end at settlement. For a new property, defects — however minor — can emerge in the months following handover. Understanding how a developer manages post-handover maintenance requests is an important, and often overlooked, part of the selection process.

Buyers should ask directly: who should they contact if a defect is identified after settlement? What is the response timeline? Who is responsible for coordinating the rectification work?

Developers who have a formal, documented defects management process demonstrate a level of organisational maturity that is reassuring for buyers, particularly those who may be living or investing from a distance.

The standards that govern residential construction in Western Australia include mandatory defect liability periods and the six-year structural warranty framework. Confirming that a developer operates within these frameworks, and that they have the resources to honour warranty claims, is a necessary part of due diligence on any Belmont townhouse development.
