# Industrial and Mechanical Degreasing: Advancing Workshop Hygiene with Botanical Solvent Chemistry

Industrial fabrication workshops, automotive garages, and commercial cycling facilities face a constant battle against heavy hydrocarbon build-up. Solidified grease, bitumen road tar, lubricating compounds, and metallic dust coat machinery housings, precision tooling, and concrete floor surfaces. For decades, maintenance teams relied on aggressive petroleum distillates, chlorinated hydrocarbons, or highly alkaline solutions to dissolve these dense industrial contaminants.

While petrochemical solvents cut through mineral grease effectively, they impose substantial environmental, health, and compliance risks. High-volatility solvents discharge hazardous volatile organic compounds (VOCs) into enclosed workshop breathing zones, creating acute respiratory risks and significant flammability hazards. Furthermore, the disposal of hydrocarbon-laden rinse runoff complicates trade-waste management for New Zealand enterprises working within strict environmental discharge limits.

Technological advancements in green chemistry have enabled facilities to replace harmful petroleum washes with high-potency bio-solvents. On contact, the solvent thins heavy petroleum greases, breaking down viscous deposits and liberating encapsulated road grit, metal filings, and particulate dirt. ## Drivetrain Engineering and Friction Reduction in Cycling Mechanics

Mechanical chain systems—including bicycle drivetrains, motorcycle linkages, and industrial roller conveyors—experience extreme frictional stresses and environmental exposure. Chains continuously gather road grit, quartz dust, and atmospheric moisture, which combine with chain lubricants to form an abrasive grinding paste. If this grit-laden sludge is not flushed from internal roller pins and bushings, component wear accelerates, leading to premature chain elongation, gear tooth rounding, and drivetrain inefficiency.

Conventional degreasing methods often cause unintended collateral damage to modern drivetrains. Solvents containing harsh kerosene or chlorinated chemicals can degrade sensitive rubber O-rings, dissolve factory-packed bearing grease, or leave an oily chemical film that prevents fresh chain lubricants from bonding correctly. Conversely, water-based degreasers that lack dedicated metal protection can promote micro-corrosion across exposed link plates.

## Best Practices for Workshop Degreasing Protocols

To maximise mechanical longevity and maintain pristine working conditions, maintenance personnel should adhere to structured degreasing procedures:

- **Target High-Wear Contact Points:** Focus degreaser application directly on roller links, chainrings, and cassette clusters. Avoid saturating sealed cartridge bearings, bottom brackets, or wheel hub shells to protect internal factory greases.
- **Allow Necessary Dwell Contact Time:** Give the botanical solvent between three and five minutes to penetrate deeply into compacted grease matrices. Agitate heavily soiled areas with a stiff nylon-bristle brush to accelerate sludge dispersion.
- **Rinse with Moderate Pressure:** Flush dislodged soils using low-pressure ambient water or damp lint-free shop cloths. High-pressure jet washers can force water past bearing dust seals, causing internal rust and bearing failure.
- **Dry Thoroughly Before Relubricating:** Ensure chain links and metallic assemblies are completely dry before applying fresh lubricant. Residual moisture impedes lubricant film formation, creating dry spots and attracting fresh road contaminants.
