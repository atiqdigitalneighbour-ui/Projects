## The Thermal Dynamics of Architectural Glass

Solar radiation entering a building envelope consists of three primary bands: ultraviolet rays, visible light, and infrared heat energy. Conventional clear float glass and standard insulated double glazing allow a major percentage of near-infrared radiation to pass directly into rooms. Once inside, this short-wave energy is absorbed by carpets, flooring, timber panelling, and furniture.

These internal surfaces convert the absorbed radiation into long-wave thermal heat, which cannot easily escape back through standard glass apertures. This phenomenon creates an uncomfortable greenhouse effect, leading to rapid indoor temperature increases throughout bright sunny days. Consequently, household heat pumps and central building cooling units must run continuously to maintain baseline comfort.

Modern spectrally selective window laminates address this thermal challenge by incorporating micro-layered ceramic or precious metal particles. These engineered layers actively reflect unwanted infrared wavelengths back outside while admitting balanced visible light. As a result, properties maintain comfortable ambient temperatures without relying heavily on continuous mechanical cooling.
