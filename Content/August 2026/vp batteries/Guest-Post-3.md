# Keeping Heavy Vehicles Moving: A Practical Guide to Truck Battery Selection and Management

The commercial transport sector in New Zealand depends on reliable vehicles. Whether a business operates refrigerated delivery trucks, long-haul freight lorries, or heavy construction equipment, a failure to start can trigger a chain of consequences that extends well beyond the vehicle itself. Understanding how heavy vehicle batteries work and how to manage them properly is essential for operators who cannot afford unnecessary downtime.

## The Scale of Power Required in Heavy Vehicles

A large diesel engine operates on compression ignition. The starter motor must turn the engine over fast enough for the cylinders to reach the temperature required for combustion. In a small passenger car, this process requires moderate cranking power. In a truck with a large displacement diesel engine, the demand is far greater.

Heavy vehicles typically use multiple batteries connected in parallel or series to provide the combined power needed. A tandem battery configuration doubles the available current, allowing the starter motor to crank a cold engine reliably even in winter conditions. The batteries must also supply power to auxiliary systems such as sleeper cab electronics, refrigeration units, and onboard telematics equipment.

## Understanding Battery Specifications for Commercial Use

Commercial vehicle batteries are rated by several key values. Cold Cranking Amps determines the battery's ability to deliver high current in cold temperatures. Reserve Capacity indicates how long the battery can power essential systems if the charging system fails. Amp-hours measure total energy storage capacity for auxiliary electrical loads.

Each of these values must be matched to the vehicle's requirements. A battery with insufficient CCA will struggle to crank a large diesel engine during cold weather. A battery with inadequate reserve capacity will leave auxiliary systems without power during extended idling or stationary operation.

When selecting replacement batteries, operators should refer to the original equipment specification rather than simply matching the physical size of the old battery. A supplier with experience in commercial vehicles can confirm the correct specification and supply a product that meets it.

## The Impact of Operating Environment on Battery Life

Commercial vehicles operate in conditions that accelerate battery degradation. Construction site vehicles face intense vibration and dust exposure. Refrigerated transport trucks run electrical loads continuously for extended periods. Long-haul vehicles experience significant temperature variation between overnight rest stops and daytime operation.

High vibration is particularly damaging. It causes the internal lead plates to shed their active material, reducing capacity and eventually leading to internal short circuits. Batteries designed for commercial applications feature reinforced plate construction and robust separators to withstand this stress.

Temperature extremes also affect performance. Cold weather reduces the available cranking power, while sustained high temperatures accelerate the chemical degradation of the battery's active material. Commercial batteries designed for demanding environments incorporate design features that improve performance across a wider temperature range.

## Proactive Battery Management for Fleets

Reactive battery replacement, waiting until a battery fails before replacing it, is the most expensive approach for a commercial operator. A battery that fails in service triggers roadside assistance callouts, driver delays, and potential load spoilage for refrigerated vehicles.

Proactive testing programmes use digital battery analysers to measure the state of health of each battery in the fleet at regular intervals. A battery approaching the end of its service life can be identified and replaced during a scheduled maintenance stop rather than at the roadside.

Many fleet operators combine battery testing with the vehicle's standard service schedule. This adds minimal time to the service process but significantly reduces the risk of unplanned battery-related breakdowns.
