## What Concrete Grinding Actually Does

Concrete grinding is a mechanical surface preparation process. Diamond-tipped tooling mounted on a rotating head is driven across the concrete surface under controlled downward pressure. As the tooling passes over the slab, it removes a controlled depth of material, abrading the surface and leaving behind a profiled texture.

The purpose of this process varies depending on what the floor is being prepared for. In some cases, grinding is used to remove an existing coating or adhesive residue. In others, it is used to level a slab that has settled unevenly, to remove surface laitance that would prevent a new coating from adhering, or to expose the concrete aggregate as part of a decorative finish.

## The Relationship Between Grinding Profile and Coating Performance

Every coating system has a recommended surface profile expressed as a Concrete Surface Profile rating. The CSP scale, developed by the International Concrete Repair Institute, ranges from CSP 1 through to CSP 10. Lower numbers indicate smoother, lightly profiled surfaces suited to thin coatings and penetrating sealers. Higher numbers indicate a more aggressively abraded surface suited to thick-build epoxy systems and cementitious overlays.

Grinding achieves profiles across the lower to mid range of the CSP scale, which covers the vast majority of commercial and residential floor coating applications. Shot blasting is used when higher CSP ratings are required. A contractor who understands the relationship between the specified coating system and the required surface profile will grind to the correct depth for the project rather than applying a generic preparation method.

## When Grinding Is Used for Floor Levelling

Uneven concrete slabs are a common issue in both older buildings and newer construction. Settlement, poor initial finishing, or damage from forklift impact can all create high spots, ridges, or surface variations that affect the performance of any overlaid floor system. Grinding can address moderate surface variation by removing high points and creating a more consistent plane.

It is important to note that grinding removes concrete from high points but does not fill low points. Where significant low spots exist, they must be filled using a cementitious levelling compound before or after grinding, depending on the project sequence. A professional assessment will identify whether levelling compound is needed and how it should be incorporated into the preparation workflow.

## Dust Management and Site Conditions

Professional concrete grinding generates a significant volume of dust if not controlled. Modern grinding equipment is used in conjunction with industrial vacuum systems that capture dust at the point of generation. This is not simply a cleanliness consideration. Uncontrolled concrete dust is a health hazard, and in occupied commercial premises, it can contaminate products, equipment, and HVAC systems.

A reputable contractor will operate with effective dust extraction as a standard part of the process, not as an optional extra. Before engaging a grinding contractor for any Christchurch commercial or residential project, confirm the dust management approach they use.
