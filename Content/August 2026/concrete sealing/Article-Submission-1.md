## New Build vs. Existing Slab: Different Challenges, Same Outcome

Polishing a new concrete slab and polishing an existing slab in a renovation or upgrade project involve different challenges. A new slab in a Rangiora residential build is typically in good condition but may need time to cure fully before polishing can begin. The contractor should assess the concrete's moisture content and confirm it is ready for grinding before committing to a timeline.

An existing slab in an older property may have surface contamination from adhesive residue, previous paint or sealer applications, or minor cracking. The grinding phase of the polishing process addresses most of these issues effectively, but the contractor should assess the slab in advance and advise on any preparatory work required before the main polishing sequence begins.

In both cases, the outcome, a smooth, seamless, professionally finished concrete floor, is achievable with the right approach and equipment.

## The Aggregate Question: What Will My Floor Look Like?

One of the most common questions from Rangiora property owners new to polished concrete is what the finished floor will look like. The answer depends largely on the aggregate in the concrete mix and the depth to which the slab is ground.

A shallow grind produces what is known as a cream finish, where the surface layer of the concrete is refined but the aggregate remains largely unexposed. This produces a more uniform, smoother-looking surface. A deeper grind exposes the stone aggregate within the mix, creating a more textured, natural appearance that shows variation in colour and aggregate distribution. The choice between these approaches is largely aesthetic and should be discussed with the contractor before work begins.

## Maintenance and Long-Term Performance in Rangiora Conditions

Rangiora's climate is consistent with the wider Canterbury region, experiencing warm, dry summers and cool winters. Polished concrete performs well in these conditions. The floor does not expand and contract with temperature changes in the way that timber can, and it does not absorb moisture during winter in the way that carpet sometimes does in poorly ventilated spaces.

Routine maintenance involves regular sweeping and damp mopping with a pH-neutral cleaner. Periodic resealing, typically every few years depending on traffic levels, keeps the surface protected and maintains its appearance. A properly maintained polished concrete floor in a Rangiora home or commercial property can remain in excellent condition for the lifetime of the building.
