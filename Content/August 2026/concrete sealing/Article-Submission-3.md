## How Professional Concrete Polishing Works

Concrete floor polishing is a mechanical process. It does not involve any applied coating or overlay. Instead, the surface of the concrete itself is refined through a series of grinding and polishing passes using diamond-bonded tooling. Each pass uses a progressively finer grit, and each removes the microscopic scratches left by the previous stage.

The process typically begins with a coarse grind to open the surface, remove existing coatings or contamination, and establish the desired aggregate exposure level. Following this, a chemical hardener is applied. This hardener reacts with compounds in the concrete to densify the surface, filling microscopic pores and increasing the concrete's resistance to abrasion and staining.

Subsequent polishing passes refine the surface through medium and fine grits until the concrete reaches the specified sheen. The final result can range from a flat, honed finish with minimal reflectivity to a high-gloss, mirror-like surface depending on the number of stages completed.

## The Three Main Finish Levels

Polished concrete finishes are commonly described by their sheen level. Understanding these options helps property owners specify what they want before work begins.

A honed or matte finish is achieved with a relatively low grit final pass. The surface is smooth and easy to clean but has minimal reflectivity. This finish is popular in high-traffic commercial environments where a more understated look is preferred and where regular cleaning is prioritised over visual impact.

A satin finish provides moderate reflectivity without the high gloss of a fully polished surface. This level is popular in residential living areas, retail environments, and hospitality venues where both performance and appearance are important.

A high-gloss finish produces a highly reflective surface that resembles polished stone. Achieving this level requires additional polishing passes and careful attention at each stage of the process. It is the most visually dramatic option and works particularly well in open-plan residential spaces and showroom environments where light reflection is an advantage.

## Aggregate Exposure: Controlling the Appearance of the Floor

One of the key decisions in a polished concrete project is the level of aggregate exposure. This is controlled by the depth of the initial grinding pass.

A minimal grind, sometimes called a cream finish, stays close to the surface of the concrete and does not expose the aggregate within the mix. The result is a relatively uniform, smooth appearance that shows the cement paste rather than the stone.

A medium grind begins to expose the edges and faces of the aggregate, producing a more textured, natural look with visible stone variation.

A full cut exposes the aggregate more completely, producing a terrazzo-like appearance that shows the colour, size, and distribution of the stone within the mix. This finish is highly dependent on the aggregate in the specific concrete mix and varies considerably from slab to slab.

## The Role of Chemical Densifiers in Achieving a Durable Finish

A step that is fundamental to the quality of any polished concrete floor but not always well understood by property owners is the application of a chemical densifier during the polishing process. Densifiers, typically lithium or sodium silicate-based solutions, penetrate the concrete surface and react chemically with the calcium compounds in the cement paste. The reaction produces a harder, denser surface that is more resistant to abrasion, less porous, and better able to hold a high-quality polish.

Skipping or inadequately applying the densifier results in a floor that appears polished initially but wears poorly over time. The surface becomes susceptible to dusting, which is the gradual release of fine concrete particles under foot traffic. A properly densified floor is significantly more durable and maintains its appearance under sustained use, making the densification step one of the most important quality indicators when evaluating a contractor's process.

## Maintenance Requirements After Polishing

One of the most practical advantages of polished concrete is its low maintenance requirement. A sealed and polished floor can be maintained with regular sweeping and damp mopping using a pH-neutral cleaner. Harsh chemical cleaners are not required and should be avoided as they can affect the sealer over time.

Periodic resealing extends the life of the surface protection and maintains the floor's appearance. Depending on traffic levels, this may be required every few years in commercial settings and less frequently in residential spaces.
