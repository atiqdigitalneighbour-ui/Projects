## What Concrete Floor Polishing Delivers

Polished concrete is achieved through a multi-stage mechanical grinding process that progressively refines the surface of the concrete slab. Diamond tooling is used at increasing grit levels until the concrete reaches the desired sheen, from a low-sheen honed finish to a high-gloss reflective surface.

The result is a seamless, hardwearing floor that requires no coatings or overlays to maintain its appearance. Polished concrete reflects ambient light, which can significantly reduce artificial lighting requirements in large commercial spaces. It is also highly resistant to abrasion and, when properly sealed with a penetrating densifier, resists surface staining effectively.

## What Epoxy Coatings Add to a Concrete Floor System

Epoxy coatings serve a different purpose. Rather than refining the surface of the concrete itself, epoxy is a resin system applied as a coating over the prepared concrete. When cured, it forms a hard, seamless, chemical-resistant layer that bonds to the substrate.

Epoxy systems are used extensively in commercial kitchens, warehouses, workshops, laboratories, medical facilities, and food production environments where hygiene, chemical resistance, and ease of cleaning are primary concerns. They can be specified in a wide range of colours and can incorporate anti-slip aggregates where required by safety standards.

## When to Use Polished Concrete and When to Use Epoxy

The decision between polished concrete and epoxy often comes down to the function of the space and the condition of the existing slab.

Polished concrete is best suited to spaces where a natural, high-end aesthetic is desired and where the slab is in good condition with suitable aggregate. Living areas, retail showrooms, office environments, and hospitality venues frequently choose polished concrete for its visual appeal and low maintenance requirements.

Epoxy is the preferred choice in environments where chemical resistance, hygiene ratings, or specific colour coding is required. Workshops, garages, food production areas, and healthcare facilities typically specify epoxy over prepared concrete.

In some commercial projects, both systems are used in different zones of the same building, with polished concrete in public-facing areas and epoxy in back-of-house service zones.

## The Importance of Correct Surface Preparation

Whether polished concrete or epoxy is the chosen system, surface preparation is the factor that most determines the outcome. Concrete must be profiled to the correct CSP rating for the chosen coating or polish, and any contamination, laitance, or weak surface material must be removed before work begins.
